## III. Energy

Energy represents a phenomenon of fundamentally different character from the continuum itself. While dt, space, ε, and μ constitute the framework of existence—immaterial, eternal, uncreated—energy manifests as **localized presence**.

### The Energieelement

The most fundamental manifestation of energy is spatial localization: a three-dimensional energy soliton, termed the **Energieelement**.

The Energieelement possesses:
- **Finite spatial extent** in all three dimensions
- **Finite energy content** (E_ee)
- **Finite manifestation time** (T_ee)

### The Necessity of Smooth Distribution

Consider an Energieelement propagating through space and encountering a plane perpendicular to its direction of motion.

The energy distribution along the direction of propagation cannot exhibit abrupt transitions. A "rectangular" distribution—energy suddenly assuming a finite value—would require instantaneous change, occurring "in no time." The derivative dE/dt, or equivalently dE/dl (since propagation occurs at V_max in empty space), would be infinite. Such an Energieelement would exert infinite force upon any particle it encountered. This does not occur in reality.

More generally, any distribution with abrupt onset or termination is impossible. Consider a triangular distribution: energy increases linearly, reaches maximum at center, then decreases linearly to zero.

At the "starting point" of such distribution, dE/dl equals zero on one side and assumes a non-zero value immediately (continuously) on the other side. This represents a change in force "in no time"—or, in the limiting case, finite_change/dt. The result is indeterminacy, indefinability, discontinuity. During dt—the fundamental framework of existence—we would have a change that is temporally undefinable, transcending time itself, transcending the possibility of existence.

The same problem arises at the maximum of the distribution, at the peak of the triangular form.

**What distribution is therefore necessary?** The energy distribution must be smoothly continuous: it must begin smoothly, reach maximum smoothly, pass through maximum smoothly, and terminate smoothly.

Visualized, this yields a smoothly continuous bump-like distribution—a smoothly continuous soliton, a smoothly continuous wave.

### The Fundamental Relation

For such a soliton, the integral of energy over its spatial extent remains constant:

$$\int E(l) \, dl = \text{constant}$$

Or equivalently, in temporal form:

$$\int E(l) \, dt = \text{constant}$$

This value is identical for all Energieelements of the same type. For Energieelements with different energy content, the same principle applies—but what determines the constant?

The simplest universal law would be: **the energy of an Energieelement multiplied by the time required for it to traverse a distance equal to its own length equals a universal constant.**

This is not evidence of external design but a consequence of fundamental simplicity. At the most basic level, existence manifests in its simplest possible form. The most straightforward law at the fundamental level is precisely what logical reasoning expects—not mystery, but clarity.

Therefore:

$$E_{ee} \cdot T_{ee} = h$$

where h is the fundamental quantum of action intrinsic to the dt-space-ε-μ continuum. [DKO-6]

### Structural Integrity

What maintains the coherence of the Energieelement?

Its very existence—its smoothly continuous energy distribution along all diameters. Throughout the interior of the Energieelement, there exists dE/dr: a force inherently directed inward, toward the center of the 3D soliton. The gradient of energy itself provides the centripetal constraint that preserves the entity's integrity as it propagates.

### Propagation

The velocity of an Energieelement through the continuum is determined by the local properties of space:

$$v_{ee} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon \mu}}$$

This relation is not imposed externally but emerges from the fundamental coupling between energy and the spatial characteristics ε and μ. In regions where ε or μ differ from their vacuum values, propagation velocity changes accordingly. [DKO-7]

### Mass-Energy Relation

From the conservation principle and the experimentally verified relation E ∝ mv², together with the requirement that finite mass yield finite energy, it follows that velocity must be bounded by V_max. At maximum energy extraction (v = V_max):

$$m = \frac{E}{V_{\text{max}}^2}$$

This is not borrowed from relativity theory but derived from first principles: the conservation of energy combined with the existence of a maximum velocity. [DKO-8]
